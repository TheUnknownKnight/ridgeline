Backcountry Route Hazard Checker

Privacy Policy & Backcountry Safety Notice

Last updated: November 22, 2026

Privacy at a glance

  • No accounts or sign-ins required
  • No analytics, ads, or crash-tracking SDKs
  • No user tracking across apps or websites
  • GPS stays on your device except for checks you request
  • Saved routes stay local in device storage
  • 100% open source under AGPL-3.0

1. Data We Do Not Collect

Ridgeline does not create user accounts, does not collect names, email addresses, phone numbers, device advertising identifiers, or behavioral analytics, and does not embed third-party advertising or tracking SDKs. We do not sell, rent, or share personal information with data brokers.

2. Location (GPS) Data

Granting location access is entirely optional. When enabled, Ridgeline uses your device's GPS strictly while the app is open to:

  • On-trail progress & ahead-of-you filtering: project your position onto the route locally on your device so you can see your trail mile, distance remaining, and upcoming hazards.
  • Trail discovery ("Near me"): query public U.S. Forest Service (USFS) and National Park Service (NPS) trail layers within your selected search radius.
  • Live route hazard checks: when you trigger an On-trail hazard check, your approximate position along the route is used to query public weather, fire, flood, air-quality, and avalanche feeds for the stretch ahead of you.

Your GPS coordinates stay on your device except for those explicit hazard checks and nearby-trail lookups you initiate. Ridgeline does not record location history on any server and does not perform background location tracking.

3. Route Files & Local Storage

GPX, KML, and GeoJSON files you open are parsed directly on your device. Only coordinate points are used for hazard checks — file names, timestamps, waypoint notes, and fitness sensor tags are stripped locally and never transmitted.

Saved routes, downloaded offline map packs, and cached hazard results are stored locally on your device (IndexedDB / Cache Storage) so you can view your last check without cell service. You can delete individual routes inside the app or remove all stored data at any time by clearing app storage or uninstalling Ridgeline.

4. Public Data Services Contacted

To check hazards and load maps or public trail geometries, Ridgeline connects to public federal and open-data services (directly from the mobile app, or through the Ridgeline web server when using the web version):

  • NIFC WFIGS (IRWIN) — active wildfire perimeters and incident locations
  • National Weather Service (NWS) & NOAA — active alerts, gridded forecasts, NWPS river gauges, HMS smoke plumes, NAQFC air-quality forecasts, and NOHRSC SNODAS snow analysis
  • EPA AirNow — hourly measured PM2.5 contours and monitors
  • Avalanche.org / National Avalanche Center — regional avalanche danger zones
  • U.S. Forest Service (USFS) & National Park Service (NPS) — public trail search and trail geometries
  • USGS LANDFIRE & Open-Meteo — terrain slope/aspect, tree canopy, weather history, and fallback air quality
  • OpenFreeMap & AWS Open Data (Mapzen Terrain) — vector basemaps and elevation shading tiles

As with any internet request, these public hosts receive standard network request metadata (such as IP address, request timestamp, and User-Agent) in accordance with their own public privacy policies. When using a hosted web instance of Ridgeline, route coordinates and route fingerprints are held only temporarily in memory for caching and are never written to a persistent database.

5. Children's Privacy

Ridgeline does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children under 13 years of age.

Backcountry Safety Notice

Ridgeline summarizes public data — it is not an official warning or navigation system. Backcountry conditions change rapidly, and public agency feeds can lag fast-moving wildfires, flash floods, storms, trail closures, or avalanche cycles.

  • Official vs. estimated data: Modeled trail conditions (snow, ice, mud, and terrain-adjusted forecasts) are deterministic physical estimates, not field observations. Never treat an estimate or missing feed as confirmation that a route is safe.
  • Verify with land managers: Always confirm current closures, permits, road access, and official bulletins directly with the managing agency (USFS, NPS, BLM, National Weather Service, and your local avalanche center) before departing.
  • Carry redundant gear: Never rely on a single phone app for backcountry navigation or emergency decisions. Carry a map, compass, sufficient power, and an independent means of emergency communication where appropriate.
